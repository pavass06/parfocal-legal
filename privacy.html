Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026 · Applies to ParFocal 1.0.0 (beta)

The short version. ParFocal has no user accounts and no servers of its own. It does not contain analytics, advertising, or tracking software of any kind. Everything ParFocal remembers about your reading is stored on your device only, and is deleted when you delete the app.

1. Who is responsible

ParFocal is published by a sole trader registered in France, identified in full on the legal notice. That person is the data controller for everything described below, within the meaning of article 4(7) of the GDPR.

For any question about this policy, or to exercise the rights described in section 9, write to contact@parfocal.fr. There is no Data Protection Officer: ParFocal is a one-person operation that carries out no large-scale or systematic monitoring, so article 37 does not require one.

2. What ParFocal stores on your device

ParFocal uses your device's local app storage. This data never leaves your phone, except the optional stats message you may choose to send (section 5). It is not backed up to any server we operate, and we have no way to read it. It covers:

  • your reading position and progress in each book;
  • your reading speed setting, in words per minute;
  • the last book you opened;
  • your music and ambient-visuals preferences, including the default ambience and the ambience chosen per book;
  • your personal library: the books you added, their reading status and favourites, and their cached metadata;
  • your completion statistics for finished books;
  • the number of words you read each day, kept for 60 days, which drives your daily goal, your streak of reading days and your best streak;
  • the random code used by the optional Send my stats button (section 5): it is created on your phone, is not derived from it, and is replaced if you reinstall the app;
  • if you choose to enter it: your own estimate of the hours you spend on your phone each day. It is typed by you, never read from your phone's system (iOS or Android), and stays on your phone;
  • whether you have completed the introduction;
  • if you turn on the daily reminder: that it is on and the time you chose. The next reminders are scheduled on your device by iOS or Android and nothing is sent to us;
  • the day you chose your current reading speed, and how many times you tapped "Later" on a suggestion to try the next speed, with the date of the last one, so that ParFocal does not suggest it again too soon;
  • for the tester feedback invitation: the date of your first launch, whether you opened the feedback form, and how many times you chose “Later”. The app knows the form was opened, never what you answered.

Deleting ParFocal from your device deletes all of it. We hold no copy. Like any app data, it may be included in your own iCloud or computer backup of the device, which you control and we never have access to.

3. What ParFocal does not do

  • No user account, sign-up, or login.
  • No advertising or third-party tracking software, and no crash-reporting software. ParFocal contains no third-party SDK. The only usage statistics it can send are the optional ones in section 5, which stay off unless you turn them on.
  • No advertising identifier, and no tracking of you across other apps or websites.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of personal data — there is none to sell.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, microphone, or location.
  • No push notifications from a server and no push token. The optional daily reminder is a local notification created on your phone: it is off until you accept it, it is at most one a day, and it shows only a generic message, or the title Claude Gueux if it is the book you are reading, on your lock screen unless you hide previews in your phone's settings.

4. Requests ParFocal makes to the internet

To find and download public-domain books, ParFocal sends read-only requests to these public catalogues:

  • gutendex.com and gutenberg.org — Project Gutenberg search and book files, and, only if gutenberg.org does not answer, gutenberg.pglaf.org, an official mirror run by Project Gutenberg that serves the same files;
  • standardebooks.org — Standard Ebooks search and editions;
  • ebooksgratuits.com — search and French-language public-domain titles;
  • openlibrary.org and covers.openlibrary.org — book metadata and cover images;
  • parfocal.fr — one small file, version.json, that holds the number of the latest beta build. ParFocal reads it to tell you when a newer version is available on TestFlight (iPhone only; Android updates come through Google Play). The request carries nothing about you or your reading.
  • stats.parfocal.workers.dev — only if you turn on the optional automatic statistics (section 5): a small server of ours, run on Cloudflare, that receives the daily statistics message.

Every request also carries a standard User-Agent header that names the app, its version and a public contact address (for example ParFocal/1.0.0 (+https://parfocal.fr; contact@parfocal.fr)), as the catalogues require. These requests carry only what is needed to find or fetch a book (your search terms and book identifiers), or, for parfocal.fr, nothing at all beyond the request itself. They contain no account, no device identifier, and nothing from section 2. ParFocal keeps no copy of your searches on any server of its own (no server exists). As with any request made from a web browser, the operator of each catalogue necessarily receives your IP address and can log it under its own policy. We have no relationship with these operators and no access to their logs.

5. Tester feedback and optional stats

These are the only places where information can deliberately leave your device, and it only happens if you choose it.

ParFocal may invite you to give feedback during the beta, and Settings contains a Share Your Feedback row. Both open a Google Form in your device's browser — you leave the app, and ParFocal sends nothing on your behalf. The form asks for your age bracket, gender, reading habits, and preferred reading format, together with your impressions of the app. It does not ask for your name or your email address.

The form is hosted by Google, so submitting it is subject to Google's privacy policy and Google receives your IP address and any information tied to a Google account you may be signed into. Answers are used for one purpose only: deciding what to build and fix during the beta. They are not published, not sold, and not combined with anything from section 2. The form does not ask for your name or e-mail address, and nothing in the app fills it in for you; the only link is one you would make yourself by typing your stats code into it. Legal basis: your consent (article 6(1)(a)), given by choosing to open and submit the form.

Answering is entirely optional. Declining changes nothing about how the app works.

Optional: send your reading statistics. During the beta, Settings has a Send my stats button. Nothing is sent unless you tap it. When you do, ParFocal shows you the exact message and opens a pre-filled e-mail to contact@parfocal.fr in your Mail app, which you can read, edit or cancel before sending. ParFocal itself contacts no server for this. The message contains only: the date of sending, the dates of the days you read over the last 14 days, the words read and the estimated minutes for each of those days, your current and best streak, the number of books started and finished, the titles of the catalogue books you opened (bundled texts and books from the catalogues in section 4) with the percentage read of each, but never the title of a book you imported yourself, your own estimate of your daily screen time as a range such as 2–3 h, or « skipped » (never a measured time) (and, in later beta builds, the number finished per difficulty level, as numbers only), your speed level, the version of the app, and a random code generated on your phone. It never contains the title of an imported book, search terms, text you read, or your name. The code is not linked to your phone or to any account; it only lets us match your messages over time, so the statistics are pseudonymous, not anonymous. Your e-mail address is visible to us when you send the message.

Legal basis: your consent, given by tapping the button (GDPR art. 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time and ask us to delete your data by writing to contact@parfocal.fr with your code. Retention: until the end of the beta plus three months, then deleted. Recipient: Julien Pavassier only; the mailbox is hosted by OVH in France (see section 7). This button exists in beta builds only.

Optional: automatic reading statistics (public version). At first launch ParFocal asks whether you want to help improve it by sending anonymous-looking reading statistics. The box is unticked by default: nothing is sent unless you tick it, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings. If you turn it on, ParFocal sends one message a day to stats.parfocal.workers.dev containing only: a random code created on your phone (not derived from it, replaced if you reinstall the app), a group code that tells us where you came from (for example a friends group), the words read on each day with the date, the date of sending, your current streak, the number of books started and finished, the number finished per difficulty level, the catalogue books you open (their catalogue identifier and the percentage read, so that we learn which classics people actually read), your speed level, your own estimate of your daily screen time as a range, the app version and the platform (iPhone or Android). It never contains the title of a book you imported yourself, any text you read, search terms, your name, your e-mail address or any device identifier, and we do not store your IP address. The code is not linked to your phone or to an account; it only lets us follow the statistics over time, so they are pseudonymous, not anonymous.

Legal basis: your consent (GDPR art. 6(1)(a)), given by ticking the box. You can withdraw it at any time by turning the setting off, and ask us to delete everything linked to your code by writing to contact@parfocal.fr with the code shown in Settings; we delete it within 30 days. Retention: 12 months after your last message, then deleted automatically. Recipient: Julien Pavassier only. Processor: Cloudflare, Inc. (United States) runs the server and the database under its Data Processing Addendum, which relies on the EU standard contractual clauses and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework; the database is created in the EU jurisdiction. As with any internet request, Cloudflare's network necessarily sees your IP address to deliver the message; we do not keep it and have no access to it.

TestFlight. If you were invited to the beta by e-mail, Apple's TestFlight service gives us your name and e-mail address as you entered them in your Apple account, and the date and number of your sessions of the app. We use them only to run the beta (send you builds, see who installed, ask you for feedback), we never put them in our code repository, and they are deleted from TestFlight at the end of the beta. Apple's privacy policy applies to what Apple itself collects. If you joined with the public link, we see none of this about you.

Google Play. If you were invited to the Android beta (internal or closed test on Google Play), Google gives us, through the Play Console, the e-mail address of the Google account you used to join the tester list, and aggregated install, version and crash statistics. We use them only to run the beta, we never put them in our code repository, and we remove you from the tester list at the end of the beta. Google's privacy policy applies to what Google itself collects. If you installed ParFocal from a direct file we gave you, Google sees nothing of it through us.

6. The parfocal.fr website

This policy covers the website you are reading as well as the app. The two are separate: the site is four static pages, and visiting it has no connection to anything the app stores on your device.

No cookies, no trackers. These pages set no cookie of any kind, contain no audience-measurement or advertising script, and load no font, image, or stylesheet from any other domain (only the beta sign-up form, below, comes from Brevo). Everything you receive comes from parfocal.fr itself. There is therefore nothing to consent to, which is why you are not asked to dismiss a banner.

Server logs. The pages are hosted by GitHub, Inc. on the GitHub Pages service, whose servers are in the United States. Like any web server, it records requests — including your IP address, the page requested, the time, and your browser's user-agent string. We do not operate those servers, do not receive those logs, and have no access to them; they are processed by GitHub under GitHub's privacy statement. This is the only transfer of data outside the European Union that visiting the site involves, and it rests on the standard contractual clauses adopted by the European Commission, which GitHub applies through Microsoft's data protection terms. Legal basis: legitimate interest (article 6(1)(f)) — serving and securing the pages you asked for.

6 bis. Beta sign-up on parfocal.fr

The home page lets you sign up for the beta. This is the only data collection on the website, and it is separate from the app, which collects nothing (sections 2 to 4).

  • Controller: the publisher of ParFocal (section 1).
  • Provider (processor): Brevo (Sendinblue SAS, a French company), which hosts the form and the list of sign-ups on servers in the European Union. The form is served from a Brevo domain (sibforms.com), so loading the page may contact that domain — the only exception to section 6 above.
  • Data collected: first name, last name, e-mail address, and phone type (iPhone or Android). No age question, no other field.
  • Purpose: sending you your beta invitation (TestFlight on iPhone, Google Play internal testing on Android) and letting you know if it is delayed. No advertising, no resale, no disclosure to anyone but Brevo.
  • Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), given by submitting the form; you may withdraw it at any time.
  • Retention: until the end of the beta plus 3 months, then deletion. You can ask for earlier deletion at contact@parfocal.fr.
  • Age: sign-up is for people aged 15 and over; below that, a parent's consent is required (section 8).
  • Your rights and the CNIL: access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction, portability (section 9). If you believe your rights are not respected, you may complain to the CNIL (cnil.fr) or your local authority.

7. If you write to us

Two things on this site invite you to send an email. Both are voluntary, and neither has a form: you write from your own mail client.

  • Support and questions — anything sent to contact@parfocal.fr. We keep the exchange for as long as it takes to answer you, and at most three years after the last message. Legal basis: legitimate interest (article 6(1)(f)) — answering someone who asked us something.
  • The beta invitation list — the install page offers to send you the installation link when the beta opens. If you ask, we keep your email address for that single purpose, until the link has been sent to you or until you ask us to remove it, and at most until the beta closes. Your address is never used for anything else, never added to a newsletter, and never passed to anyone. Legal basis: your consent (article 6(1)(a)), given by writing to us — withdrawable at any time by replying to say so.

These messages are stored in a mailbox hosted by OVH SAS in France, on the Zimbra service. They stay in the European Union.

8. Children

ParFocal is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them. The catalogues ParFocal reads are general-purpose libraries of classic literature and are not curated for children. The optional beta features that send something (section 5) are meant for adults; in France, a person under 15 needs a parent's agreement to use them.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, correct, and erase your personal data, and to object to its processing. In practice:

  • For everything in section 2 — it is on your device and under your sole control. Deleting the app erases it. We cannot access it, so we cannot retrieve, export, or delete it for you.
  • For a feedback form answer — write to the address in section 1 describing when you answered, and the response will be deleted. Because the form collects no identifier, that description is the only way to locate it.
  • For the optional automatic statistics (section 5) — turn the setting off in Settings to stop sending, and write to the address in section 1 with the code shown in Settings to have everything linked to it deleted within 30 days.
  • For an email you sent us (section 7) — write to the same address and we will send you a copy of what we hold, correct it, or delete it. Removal from the beta invitation list is immediate and needs no reason.
  • For the server logs (section 6) — they are GitHub's, not ours. Exercise your rights over them directly with GitHub, whose privacy statement explains how.

You may also object to a processing based on legitimate interest, ask for it to be restricted, and — where a processing rests on consent or a contract and is carried out by automated means — ask for your data in a portable format. In practice, the only data we hold that this can apply to is an email you sent us.

We answer within one month. There is nothing to prove your identity with, so a request is honoured if it comes from the address concerned.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority — in France, the CNIL, 3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07.

10. Changes to this policy

If ParFocal ever starts collecting data — for example if accounts or purchases are added — this page will be updated before that version ships, and the date at the top will change. This page is versioned, so its history is public.
